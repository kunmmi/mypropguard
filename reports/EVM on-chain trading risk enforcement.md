# Guards can block trades, not score P&L

The core finding is blunt: **on EVM chains, an on-chain gate that blocks a non-compliant trade regardless of which front-end signs it is real and buildable today** — Safe Guards, ERC-7579 hooks, and EIP-7702 delegates all give a third party unconditional, revert-based veto power over every transaction an account sends, closing exactly the bypass hole that makes MyPropGuard's current Hyperliquid "check rules then cosign" model defeatable on Hyperliquid's own UI. But the mechanism only solves half the problem. The *hard* part — knowing a position's current notional size, leverage, and today's realized P&L cheaply and trustlessly enough to compare against a limit on every single trade — is not solved by any of these account-abstraction primitives, and no shipped product anywhere in the EVM ecosystem has published a design that does it convincingly. Safe's own Allowance Module, the most mature "unbypassable" spending control on Ethereum, only checks a cumulative token amount against two hard-coded transfer shapes — it cannot even see a swap, let alone a leveraged position. Zodiac Roles can scope arbitrary function parameters on a known contract, which gets close to "max position size per trade," but nobody has extended it to cumulative cross-trade state like leverage or daily P&L. The realistic path for MyPropGuard Onchain is therefore narrow but real: start with the two rules that need no P&L or oracle data at all (**max trade count/cooldown** and a simple, parameter-scoped **max position size** on a specific, pre-integrated perps protocol), treat **max daily loss** and true **max leverage** as a phase-two problem requiring either an owned on-chain position ledger or an accepted off-chain attestation, and pick **Safe + Zodiac Guard** as the mechanism — not ERC-4337 and not EIP-7702 — because it is the most audited, lowest-engineering-risk option for a v1 that only needs to reach one or two named protocols on BSC and Ethereum.

## Three mechanisms can all revert a bad trade, but only one is mature enough to ship on

Three distinct ways exist to put a third party's revert logic between a trader's signature and a trade's execution, and the research notes converge on the same ranking for a v1 product. A **Safe Guard** is a contract the Safe calls via `checkTransaction` immediately before, and `checkAfterExecution` immediately after, every `execTransaction` — a revert in either call reverts the whole Safe transaction, and this has worked, unmodified, since Safe v1.3.0 ([Safe Guards docs](https://docs.safe.global/advanced/smart-account-guards)). Critically, Safe's v1.5.0 release closed the one real bypass in this model: a separate `moduleGuard`/`checkModuleTransaction` hook now covers module-initiated transactions too, so a bot or automation module can no longer route around the Guard ([Safe Foundation: Introducing Safe v1.5.0](https://safefoundation.org/blog/introducing-safe-v1-5-0-module-guards-enhanced-smart-account-features)). The second mechanism is **ERC-4337/ERC-7579 modular smart accounts**: validation and execution are strictly separated through a singleton EntryPoint, and a hook module can intercept and revert on the real call data both pre- and post-execution, generalizing the Guard idea across any ERC-7579-compliant wallet (Safe's own AA module, Biconomy Nexus, ZeroDev Kernel, Alchemy's Modular Account) rather than being Safe-specific ([ERC-7579 Modules](https://erc7579.com/modules)). The third is **EIP-7702**, live on Ethereum mainnet since the Pectra upgrade on May 7, 2025, which lets an existing, already-funded EOA sign an authorization that installs a delegate contract's code at the EOA's own address and nonce — solving the "trader doesn't want to migrate to a new address" objection outright ([EIP-7702 spec](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-7702)).

For MyPropGuard specifically, Safe+Guard wins on three practical grounds the notes substantiate directly. First, maturity: Safe Guards have shipped since 2023, Zodiac Roles is in production use by major DAOs (ENS completed a "Roles Modifier V2" migration of its own treasury permissions), and the ecosystem's hardening work (module guards, decoder depth limits) reflects years of adversarial pressure that neither ERC-7579 hooks for trading nor EIP-7702 trading delegates have yet received ([discuss.ens.domains](https://discuss.ens.domains/t/ep-5-12-roles-modifier-v2-migration-updates-to-endowment-permissions/19173)). Second, gas: a plain Safe `execTransaction` with a Guard adds roughly 50k–80k gas over an equivalent EOA call, versus 60k–120k extra for a full ERC-4337 UserOperation; EIP-7702 is cheapest in principle (~42k gas overhead) but the spec's own security considerations warn that "a poorly implemented delegate can allow a malicious actor to take near complete control," because unlike a bolt-on Guard, a 7702 delegate *becomes* the account's entire logic ([BuildBear: ERC-4337 vs EIP-7702 Comparison](https://www.buildbear.io/blog/eip-4337-7702-comparison); [EIP-7702 spec](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-7702)). Third, chain availability: ERC-4337 and Safe require no hard fork and have run on every EVM chain since 2023, while EIP-7702 requires a chain to have adopted Pectra or an equivalent fork — confirmed only for Ethereum mainnet and Arbitrum in the research gathered, with **BSC's EIP-7702 status unconfirmed** and needing direct verification before any BSC-side EIP-7702 plan is finalized. ERC-4337/7579 remains the better long-run answer if MyPropGuard wants cross-wallet portability (a module written once runs on Biconomy, ZeroDev, and Safe's own AA module), but that portability is a phase-two optimization, not a v1 requirement, and it costs more gas and more unaudited surface area than a Safe Guard does today.

## A Guard sees raw calldata, not a leverage number — and that is the real constraint on scope

Every mechanism above shares one hard limit that the "which mechanism" question obscures: **a Guard, hook, or delegate only ever receives raw, undecoded `to`/`value`/`data` bytes** — there is no generic, protocol-agnostic layer that tells it "this call opens a 10x leveraged position." Safe's own tutorial demonstrates only trivial checks and provides no calldata-decoding guidance; recognizing "this is a GMX position-open call for $50k notional at 10x" requires hand-written, per-protocol ABI-decoding logic that must be rewritten whenever a protocol upgrades its contracts ([Building a Guard for Safe Smart Account](https://docs.safe.global/advanced/smart-account-guards/smart-account-guard-tutorial)). Zodiac's **Roles Modifier** is the closest existing primitive to a generalized version of this: it can scope a role down to specific parameter conditions on a known function of a known contract, and its "allowances" feature adds running, rate-limited budgets enforced across many calls — but every documented production use is treasury payments (destination, token, amount, frequency), never derivatives parameters, and no cited source shows anyone extending it to leverage or cumulative position state ([Zodiac Roles Modifier docs](https://docs.roles.gnosisguild.org/)). This is not a minor gap — it means **the Guard has to be written and audited per target protocol**, not once, generically.

Protocol compatibility compounds this. GMX's core "Classic Trading" path (direct calls to its router/exchange contracts) does not gate on EOA-ness and is explicitly documented as usable from a Safe or other smart-contract wallet — but GMX's newer "Express Trading"/"One-Click Trading" and "GMX Account" features, which route through Gelato relayers, are explicitly *not* compatible with smart wallets yet ([GMX Trading overview docs](https://docs.gmx.io/docs/trading/overview/)). Synthetix Perps V3's NFT-based account-permission model is architecturally AA-friendly by design intent, but no source confirms the absence of EOA-only checks in its position-modifying functions. Gains Network (gTrade) and BSC-native ApolloX have no public evidence either way on smart-wallet compatibility of their core trading functions — this would need direct source review before committing engineering time to either. dYdX v4 is a sovereign Cosmos app-chain, not EVM, and is out of scope entirely; Hyperliquid's HIP-3 builder-deployed perps run on HyperCore, a non-EVM precompiled state machine, so the Safe/ERC-4337 question doesn't map onto it even via HyperEVM. The practical implication: **a v1 cannot be "any perps protocol" — it has to be one or two specific, pre-vetted integrations**, starting with GMX Classic Trading (the only protocol in this set with an affirmative compatibility confirmation) and treating every other named protocol as requiring its own direct contract-level verification pass before integration.

## The oracle/P&L problem, not the gate mechanism, is what makes two of the four rules hard

The single biggest asymmetry in this research is between rules that need only the *current transaction's own parameters* and rules that need *the account's accumulated state across many trades plus a trusted price*. **Max trade count and cooldown** need neither: a Guard just reads and increments an on-chain counter/timestamp, a cold SLOAD plus a comparison, costing on the order of a few thousand gas — negligible next to a 150k–200k gas swap ([engineering estimate synthesizing RareSkills and GitHub Gist gas data](https://rareskills.io/post/chainlink-price-feed-contract)). This rule is **easy**: fully on-chain, no oracle, shippable in v1 on both BSC and Ethereum with no meaningful gas concern, since BSC's gas prices (~0.05–0.1 Gwei) and 2026's unusually low Ethereum mainnet prices (~1.1 Gwei) make even a few thousand extra gas immaterial in dollar terms ([BscScan Gas Tracker](https://bscscan.com/gastracker); [Etherscan Gas Tracker](https://etherscan.io/gastracker)).

**Max position size**, if scoped as "this single trade's notional must not exceed X," is also tractable without an oracle if the trade is denominated in a stablecoin or a token amount, directly analogous to how Zodiac Roles already scopes "spend up to N tokens on this function" today — this makes it **medium difficulty**: it requires per-protocol calldata decoding (not generic) but no price feed, so it belongs in v1 for the one or two protocols MyPropGuard chooses to integrate first. It becomes **hard** the moment "position size" must be expressed in USD against a volatile collateral asset, because that requires a price read on every check — a Chainlink push-feed read adds a cheap few thousand gas, but a Uniswap V3 TWAP `observe()` call costs 65,000–92,000 gas, which can rival or exceed the cost of the trade itself and risks blowing through ERC-4337's default 50,000-gas validation-phase ceiling if placed in the wrong phase ([GitHub Gist: Uniswap V3 Oracle Gas Tests](https://gist.github.com/alexroan/71b38d387ed2a86bf3abdf3acd0f8415); [Eco: ERC-4337 Explained](https://eco.com/support/en/articles/14856079-what-is-account-abstraction-erc-4337-in-plain-english)).

**Max leverage** and **max daily loss** are both **hard-to-infeasible** for a structural reason that recurs across every research note: unlike Aave's health factor (a self-contained calculation because collateral and debt already live in the protocol's own storage) or GMX's event-sourced realized P&L (readable on-chain only for *closed* positions, via the `ClosePosition` event), MyPropGuard sits *outside* the venue it wraps and has no native ledger of "this trader's leverage right now" or "this trader's realized P&L today" to read ([DeepWiki: GMX contracts](https://deepwiki.com/grass-dev-pa/gmx-contracts-015); [Aave V3 Risk Parameter Methodology](https://chaoslabs.xyz/resources/chaos_aave_risk_param_methodology.pdf)). Three paths exist to close this gap, and none is free: (a) replay all historical events on-chain each check — gas-prohibitive past a handful of trades per day; (b) MyPropGuard deploys and atomically updates its own position-accounting contract on every guarded trade, paired with a price oracle to mark positions to market — fully on-chain and trustless given the oracle, but it is nontrivial engineering MyPropGuard would own and audit itself, essentially rebuilding an Aave-style ledger from scratch per integrated protocol; or (c) an off-chain attester (MyPropGuard's own backend) computes P&L and signs an attestation the Guard verifies — architecturally standard (this is exactly how Pyth's pull-oracle model works) but it reintroduces precisely the trusted, available third party the "unbypassable" design goal is meant to eliminate, and it fails in one of two ways during a backend outage: fail-open (defeats the purpose) or fail-closed (a backend outage becomes a trading outage) ([TokenToolHub: AI and Blockchain Oracles](https://tokentoolhub.com/ai-and-blockchain-oracles/)). A fourth, cheaper hybrid — a keeper (Chainlink CRE, successor to the now-sunsetting Chainlink Automation and Gelato Automate as of 2026) periodically computes P&L/leverage off-chain and flips a cheap on-chain "frozen" flag the Guard checks on every trade — moves the expensive computation out of the hot path, but it only catches threshold breaches at the keeper's polling interval, leaving the same bounded latency gap that liquidation-bot-dependent protocols like Aave and GMX already live with ([FluidRWA: Chainlink Automation vs Gelato](https://www.fluidrwa.com/blog/chainlink-automation-vs-gelato-vs-openzeppelin)). None of this is a flaw unique to MyPropGuard's design — it is the same honest tradeoff every DeFi oracle and attestation system makes, but it does mean the "unbypassable, no cooperating third party" marketing claim cannot be literally true for leverage or P&L-based rules without MyPropGuard owning pattern (b).

## No one has shipped this, which is opportunity, not a red flag

The prior-art research is unusually clear on one point: genuinely preventive, trading-specific, front-end-independent circuit breakers do not appear to exist as shipped EVM products. Safe's Allowance Module — the most mature, widely-deployed preventive spending-limit primitive on Ethereum — is deliberately scoped to two hard-coded transfer shapes and cannot see a trade at all ([github.com/safe-global/safe-modules](https://github.com/safe-global/safe-modules/tree/main/modules/allowances)). DeFi Saver and Instadapp, the two most prominent DeFi "position risk automation" products, are both reactive bot-triggered systems that submit a corrective transaction *after* a monitored condition is crossed — architecturally the same class of pattern as MyPropGuard's current Hyperliquid model, just automated rather than human-cosigned, and both chose this over a synchronous pre-trade gate ([blog.defisaver.com](https://blog.defisaver.com/automated-strategies-technical-overview/); [guides.instadapp.io](https://guides.instadapp.io/features/protection-automation)). "On-chain prop firms" like Hypernova market themselves on verifiable, immutable rules, but the one detailed third-party review found treats their actual trade-level enforcement mechanism as undocumented and unresolved, and the one confirmed Hypernova control (a $10K/day payout cap) is a withdrawal limit, not a position/leverage circuit breaker ([defiprime.com](https://defiprime.com/onchain-prop-firms)). DAO treasury tooling (Safe Guards + Zodiac Roles) is the most mature preventive pattern that exists, but it has never been extended, in any documented case, to derivatives trading semantics — only to vendor payments. The consistent explanation across every example is the same oracle/state-tracking problem described above, not lack of demand or prohibitive gas cost (gas, per the research, is a minor factor at 2026's low price levels on both chains). This is a genuinely open niche: nobody has built and shipped the thing MyPropGuard would be building, which cuts both ways — no competitor has a head start, but there is also no audited reference implementation to build on, meaning the Guard logic for position-size and (eventually) leverage/P&L rules will need to be written and audited from scratch per integrated protocol.

## Conclusion: narrow v1, Safe+Guard, two rules first

This is worth pursuing, but only as a deliberately narrow first version, not a general "trade through any front-end with full risk enforcement" product. The recommended v1 is a Safe + Zodiac Guard wrapping one pre-vetted protocol (GMX Classic Trading, the only one in this research with confirmed smart-wallet compatibility) on whichever of BSC or Ethereum has the lower integration friction, enforcing exactly two rules at launch: **max trade count/cooldown** (trivial, fully on-chain, ship immediately) and **max position size per trade** denominated in a stable asset or token amount (medium effort, requires protocol-specific calldata decoding but no oracle). Max leverage and max daily loss should be explicitly scoped out of v1 and messaged as a roadmap item requiring either an owned on-chain position ledger (the defensible, more "unbypassable" option, but real engineering) or an accepted off-chain attestation with disclosed trust assumptions (faster to ship, but a step back toward the same "cooperating signer" model MyPropGuard is trying to move beyond on Hyperliquid). The strategic payoff of even this narrow v1 is real: it would let MyPropGuard credibly claim — for the first time anywhere in this research — that a trader cannot bypass max-position-size and trade-frequency limits by switching front-ends, something no competitor product currently demonstrates with public, auditable enforcement at the contract level. EIP-7702 should be revisited specifically once BSC's hard-fork status is confirmed, since it is the only mechanism that lets an existing, reputationally-established trading EOA gain this protection without migrating addresses — but it should wait until after the Safe+Guard v1 proves the risk-logic model, given its higher single-point-of-failure risk ("a poorly implemented delegate can allow near complete control") and its narrower, unconfirmed chain support today.
